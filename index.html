A Community in Mourning After Local Man Loses ‘The Game’

John Milas’s defeat has sent shockwaves through the community, leaving friends, neighbors and fellow residents questioning what comes next.

Listen · 1:17 min
Share full article
By Owen Russell
Reporting from Chapel Hill, N.C.

Nov. 17, 2026, 7:42 a.m. ET

John Milas had last lost just three days earlier, and since then he had managed, for a whopping total of three days, to do what millions of others could not. He had remained unaware, or perhaps simply undisturbed, by the invisible competition unfolding around him. But on Tuesday, that streak came to an abrupt end.

John Milas lost The Game.

The circumstances surrounding the loss remain unclear, though witnesses say the news spread quickly throughout the local community. What began as an ordinary day soon became a moment of collective devastation, as friends and acquaintances came to terms with the reality that another member of their community had fallen.

For those unfamiliar with The Game, the rules are deceptively simple: thinking about The Game means losing The Game. Everyone is required to participate, cannot officially withdraw, and is generally unable to avoid losing indefinitely. The only consolation is that announcing one’s loss forces others to lose as well.

It is a system that has quietly affected countless lives, often without warning.

Here’s what is known about the loss as the community takes stock:

A Neighbor Remembered

Friends described Mr. Milas as an otherwise ordinary member of the community, whose loss has raised difficult questions about the nature of the competition and whether anyone can truly be considered safe.

“I’m devastated, honestly,” said Finn King, a close friend of Mr. Milas’s since middle school. “John was one of my best friends. He’d lost literally three days ago, so he was on a streak of, like, three days. But I really thought he was going to make it.”

Mr. King said he found out by text. “I was just sitting on my couch and my phone buzzed,” he said. “And then I lost too, obviously. That’s the worst part. I went down with him.”

Others expressed frustration that the incident could have been prevented, while acknowledging that the rules leave little room for intervention.

A Chain Reaction

By Tuesday afternoon, the effects of Mr. Milas’s loss had extended well beyond his immediate circle. Several residents reported experiencing losses of their own after hearing the news, creating what some have described as a chain reaction of unprecedented proportions.

Despite the widespread disappointment, the community has begun the difficult process of moving forward. Some have called for greater awareness of The Game, while others believe that discussing the incident only risks prolonging its consequences.

“I’m not mad at him,” Mr. King said. “I just keep thinking about it. I don’t really know what to do with myself right now.”

For now, there are few answers. Mr. Milas has lost, the community has suffered, and countless others have been unwillingly reminded of a competition they had spent years trying to forget.

There is, however, one certainty.

“You just lost The Game.”
